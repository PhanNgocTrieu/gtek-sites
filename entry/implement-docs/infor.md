4.0  Home Page
The home page is the most important page of the site. A first-time visitor must understand within five seconds: who Gtek is, what Gtek does, and how to make contact. The page is structured as five vertically stacked sections.
4.1  Hero Section
Full-width banner at the top of the page. Background image is a high-quality geotechnical site photo (e.g., a drilling rig, dam structure, or slope inspection) with a dark overlay so white text remains legible. The hero contains a headline, supporting line, and a single call-to-action button.

The About page tells visitors who Gtek is and, more importantly, who works at Gtek. For a startup competing against established firms, the named team is the strongest credibility asset — clients hire people, not logos.
5.1  Company Narrative
Two to three short paragraphs at the top of the page. Avoid corporate filler; focus on facts and positioning.
DRAFT COPY — COMPANY NARRATIVE
Paragraph 1 — Who we are:
Gtek Engineering Inc. is a geotechnical consulting firm founded in Winnipeg, Manitoba. We provide engineering services across dam safety, mining, foundations, and slope stability, drawing on more than 25 years of combined senior experience.

Paragraph 2 — How we work:
We are deliberately small. Every project is led by a Principal and supported by a focused technical team. This structure lets us respond quickly, control quality at every stage, and build long-term working relationships with our clients.

Paragraph 3 — Where we work:
Gtek serves clients across Canada from our Winnipeg office, with active projects in Manitoba and surrounding provinces. We undertake assignments at any stage — from desktop study through construction monitoring and long-term performance review.

6.0  Projects Page
The Projects page demonstrates capability through real examples. For a startup, this presents a practical challenge: Gtek itself has limited project history, but its principal personnel have extensive experience. The page should resolve this honestly — by featuring projects that Gtek personnel have delivered, with clear attribution of role.
6.1  Page Structure
Project cards arranged in a responsive grid (typically 3 columns on desktop, 1 column on mobile). For launch, 4 to 6 representative projects is sufficient; the goal is quality of presentation, not exhaustive listing.
6.2  Project Card Layout
Image	Project photo or rendering, landscape orientation, ~16:9 aspect ratio
Project name	Bold, on one line
Sector tag	Small colored label (e.g., 'Dam Safety', 'Mining')
Client	Public reference if permitted; otherwise sector-only ('Confidential mining client')
Location	City, Province (or region)
Scope summary	1–2 sentences describing Gtek's role and the technical scope
6.3  Sourcing Project Content
The strongest source of project content is the project experience already documented in Wayne Wong's Hatch-branded CV (currently being transferred into the Gtek CV format). Each entry in that CV becomes a candidate project card, with the role attribution adjusted appropriately.
•	For projects delivered while at a previous firm, attribute the role clearly: 'Wayne Wong served as Lead Geotechnical Engineer while at [Firm]. The project is shown here to illustrate Gtek's capability.'
•	For projects delivered as Gtek, attribute the role to Gtek directly.
•	Confirm publication permissions before naming any client. When in doubt, generalize ('a Manitoba hydroelectric utility').

6.5  Optional Filtering
If desired, a horizontal filter bar above the project grid allows visitors to filter by sector (Dam Safety, Mining, Foundations, Slope Stability). This is a 'nice to have' for v1 — if the project list is short (under ~10 entries), filtering adds little value and can be deferred to a later release.

The Contact page should make it as easy as possible for a prospective client to reach Gtek. Three elements: contact details, an embedded map, and a contact form. All three appear on the same page.

7.2  Embedded Map
Google Maps embed showing the Winnipeg office location. Provides geographic context and signals that Gtek is a real, located firm. Standard Google Maps iframe; no special setup required.
7.3  Contact Form
Simple form with the minimum fields required to triage an inquiry. Long forms reduce submission rates.
Name	Required
Email	Required
Company	Optional
Subject	Required — dropdown: General Inquiry / Project Inquiry / Career / Other
Message	Required — multi-line text area

Form submissions should be routed to a designated Gtek inbox (suggested: info@gtek.ca, with auto-forward to Wayne and Kevin during the early phase). A confirmation message should appear on submission, and an automatic acknowledgement email is recommended.
7.4  Anti-Spam
A spam-protection mechanism is essential. Recommended approach: Google reCAPTCHA v3 (invisible to legitimate users) or a simple honeypot field. Without protection, a public contact form will receive significant spam volume within weeks of launch.
